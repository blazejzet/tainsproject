TrainsCoding

An 8-bit coding playground for kids aged 5–10. You program characters by building trains: the locomotive is the event, and the wagons are the commands.

iPadOS 26 macOS 26 English & Polish No reading required

The idea

A program is a train

Every character on the stage owns some trains. A train always starts with a locomotive, which says when the train runs: when ▶ is pressed, when the character is tapped, when it bumps into a wall, when a message arrives, and so on.

Behind the locomotive come the wagons. Each wagon is one command: move, turn, say something, wait, repeat, switch gravity on, add 1 to a variable. The train runs its wagons from left to right.

Wagons are colour-coded by category and painted with a pixel icon, so children can read a program without reading words. While the program runs, the wagon that is working right now lights up.

What children learn

Six kinds of wagon, six big ideas

Events

Red locomotives. Programs react to the world: taps, buttons, bumps, timers and messages.

Motion

Blue wagons. Move in tiles, turn in degrees, jump to a spot, get a physics kick.

Looks

Purple wagons. Speech bubbles, costumes, colours, size, fading and a drawing pen.

Control

Orange wagons. Waiting, loops, if/else, messages, clones, and winning or losing.

Physics

Green wagons. Gravity, weight, solid and ghost bodies, magnets, joints and dragging.

Data

Yellow wagons. Four number boxes A–D, hearts, and showing a score on the stage.
